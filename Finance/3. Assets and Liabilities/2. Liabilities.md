# Liabilities

A liability is an obligation owed to another person or organization. Examples include money borrowed and amounts due for goods or services already received.

Borrowing money creates both an asset, the money received, and a liability, the obligation to repay it. Receiving borrowed money does not by itself make the borrower richer.

Paying an obligation uses an asset to reduce a liability. The thing owned and the obligation owed are separate, even when they relate to the same purchase. For example, someone buys a car with a loan. The car is an asset they own, and the loan is a liability they owe. Paying off the loan reduces their cash and the loan, but the car is still theirs.
