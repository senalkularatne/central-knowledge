### Foundational Additions

- Interest and compounding: interest paid or earned, including interest on earlier interest.
- Assets, liabilities, and net worth: what is owned, what is owed, and the difference.
- Supply and demand: how availability and willingness to buy affect prices.
- Revenue, expenses, profit, and cash flow: sales, costs, what remains after costs, and actual cash moving in and out.
- Financial statements: what the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement each show.
- Shares and company value: share count, market capitalization (share price multiplied by shares outstanding), dividends, stock splits, buybacks, and dilution.
- Investment returns: percentage gains and losses, growth multiples, and returns including dividends.
- Diversification: spreading investments rather than depending on one company; why this reduces some risks but does not eliminate losses.
- Valuation basics: the difference between a quoted price and what an investment may be worth, without detailed valuation models yet.
