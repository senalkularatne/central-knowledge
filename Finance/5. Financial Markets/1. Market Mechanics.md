# Market Mechanics

Market mechanics describes how buying and selling actually happen. This note introduces the basic trading vocabulary, not trading strategies.

## Brokers and Commissions

A broker handles purchases and sales for clients. A brokerage account is the account through which a client holds investments and submits trading instructions.

A commission is a fee charged for carrying out a transaction. A trade advertised as commission-free can still involve other costs, including the difference between buying and selling prices described below.

## Orders and Order Books

An order is an instruction to buy or sell:

- A market order seeks to trade at the available prices. It does not guarantee the price last shown on a screen.
- A limit order sets the highest price a buyer will pay or the lowest price a seller will accept. It may not execute.

An order book records outstanding buying and selling orders at different prices and quantities on a trading venue. A bid is a price a buyer offers; an ask is a price a seller requests. The gap between the best bid and best ask is the bid-ask spread.

## Liquidity

An investment is liquid when it can be bought or sold readily without substantially changing its price. An illiquid investment may take longer to sell or require a worse price. Liquidity does not mean that an investment is safe or that its price cannot fall.

## Market Makers

A market maker is a firm that stands ready to buy and sell at quoted prices, trading for its own account. This can help other investors find someone to trade with. A market maker takes on the risk of price changes in what it holds; the spread is not guaranteed profit.

## Exchanges and OTC Trading

An exchange is an organized trading venue with rules for participants and transactions. The next note, [Exchanges](2.%20Exchanges.md), provides optional examples.

OTC means over-the-counter: trading outside an exchange, often through networks of dealers. A dealer trades for its own account rather than solely arranging a trade for someone else. OTC is a way of trading, not a synonym for penny stocks or for an absence of regulation.

## Bullish and Bearish

Bullish means expecting prices to rise; bearish means expecting them to fall. These words can describe a view about one investment or a wider market. They describe an expectation, not proof of what will happen.

## Market Open and Close

"Wall Street opens" or "the market closes" usually refers to the start or end of the regular U.S. stock trading session, not every financial market worldwide.

As of 2026, regular New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and Nasdaq stock trading is generally 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time on trading weekdays, with holidays and some early closes. Eastern Time changes with daylight saving time, so it is not always the same UTC offset.

Some trading takes place before or after that session. Availability depends on the broker and venue; fewer participants and wider spreads can make those trades harder. Submitting an order while the regular market is closed does not guarantee immediate execution.
