# Market Regulation

Market regulators oversee financial products, trading venues, and the firms that serve investors. Their responsibilities differ by product and can overlap.

Self-regulation means that an industry organization sets and enforces rules under government oversight. Public-company disclosures are information that companies are required to make public. Municipal securities concern borrowing by state and local governments and related public bodies.

## United States

- **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC):** Oversees securities markets, public-company disclosures, securities exchanges, and many investment professionals.
- **Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC):** Oversees commodity futures, options on futures, and [swaps](../4.%20Financial%20Instruments/2.%20Derivatives/0.%20Derivatives.md#other-forms-not-yet-covered) markets.
- **Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA):** A self-regulatory organization overseen by the SEC. It writes and enforces rules for member broker-dealers and their registered representatives.
- **Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB):** Writes rules for firms and professionals in the municipal securities market; the SEC and other regulators enforce those rules.
- **State securities regulators:** Enforce state securities laws and handle matters such as state-level registration and investor complaints.

The product matters: a stock option is generally a securities product, while an option on a futures contract is generally overseen by the CFTC. Some products and activities involve more than one regulator, so confirm the specific rules before relying on a simple category.
