# Non-Derivative Instruments

This branch covers ordinary forms of money, repayment claims, and ownership interests, rather than derivative contracts based on a separate underlying price or measure.

The children are grouped by instrument form. This is a foundational selection informed by the IMF's instrument classification, not a complete reproduction of its statistical framework or a universal legal classification.

Currency and deposits establish the forms of money first. Loans introduce repayment terms before debt securities. Ownership interests then contrast owning with lending.

## Cash Terminology

In IAS 7 financial statements, cash includes physical cash and demand deposits, meaning bank deposits available on demand. Currency and deposits are still different instrument forms, so they have separate notes here.

Cash equivalents are certain short-term investments that can readily be converted into a known amount of cash with insignificant risk of a change in value. This describes qualifying characteristics, not a separate instrument form.

Some introductory descriptions call non-derivatives "cash instruments." That phrase does not mean that ordinary stocks and bonds are spendable cash.

## Classification Boundaries

Debt is broader than debt securities. Deposits and loans can also be debt claims. The IMF's broad debt classification includes currency and deposits, so adding "Cash" beside "Debt Instruments" as supposedly exclusive categories would be misleading.

This branch uses the specific instrument forms instead. Instruments with combined or unusual terms need separate examination; their names alone do not settle their classification.
