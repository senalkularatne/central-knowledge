# Stocks

A stock represents ownership in a company; a share is a unit of that ownership. "Company shares" and "stocks" refer to the same subject here.

Ownership can produce a return through changes in share price and through dividends, which are distributions a company makes to shareholders. Neither a rising price nor a dividend is guaranteed.

## Share Rights

A share class is a group of shares with specified rights. Common and preferred describe different rights, not whether the company is publicly traded.

- Common Stock: often carries voting rights and a claim on what remains after the company's obligations and prior claims are met.
- Preferred Stock: generally has priority over common stock for specified distributions and usually has limited or no voting rights. Payment and voting terms vary; a preferred dividend is not automatically guaranteed.

These are introductory descriptions. The actual rights and classification depend on the terms of the shares.

## Public and Private Equity

Publicly traded shares can be bought and sold in public markets.

Private equity is ownership in companies whose shares are not publicly traded. Investors may own it directly or through a private equity fund that pools their money to acquire stakes in businesses, sometimes buying a public company and taking it private. Selling such an investment can be much harder than selling a publicly traded stock.

Public/private ownership and common/preferred rights answer different questions. They are not four mutually exclusive stock types.

## Related Company and Receipt Forms

- A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. This describes an organization, not a share class. Some REITs are publicly traded; others are not.
- An American Depositary Receipt (ADR) is a certificate issued by a U.S. depositary bank representing shares in a foreign company. It gives investors another way to hold an interest connected to those shares; it is not a new common/preferred share class.
