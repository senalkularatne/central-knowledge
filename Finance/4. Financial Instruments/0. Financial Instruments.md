# Financial Instruments

A financial instrument establishes financial rights and obligations between parties. For example, a loan gives the lender a right to repayment and the borrower an obligation to repay; a company share gives its holder an ownership interest.

This reference includes currency, deposits, loans, shares, and derivative contracts. Currency is itself a financial asset and is the means of settling many other financial obligations.

## Securities

Security is a legal classification, not simply another word for anything that can be traded. In the United States, the definition in 15 U.S.C. 77b(a)(1) includes stocks, bonds, and specified options, among other instruments. This is an introductory example of a legal definition, not a complete legal test.

A stock and a stock option can both be securities even though one is non-derivative and the other is a derivative. Securities therefore do not form a third, separate branch beside Non-Derivative Instruments and Derivatives.

Likewise, a currency-related instrument can be non-derivative or derivative. The currency it concerns is a different classification question from the form of its contract.
