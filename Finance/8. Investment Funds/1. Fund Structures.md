# Fund Structures

This note introduces common U.S. fund arrangements. Publicly offered and privately offered funds differ in how their interests are offered and who can invest. The examples below are not a complete catalogue of fund structures.

## Publicly Offered Funds

The following examples differ in how investors buy and sell their shares.

### Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)

An ETF is a fund whose shares trade during the trading day, like a stock. It may hold stocks, bonds, or other investments. Buying one ETF does not automatically provide broad [diversification](../5.%20Risk%20and%20Return/2.%20Portfolios.md#diversification); some funds focus on a single industry or a narrow strategy.

The trading price can differ from the fund's [net asset value per share](../6.%20Financial%20Instruments/1.%20Non-Derivative%20Instruments/5.%20Equity%20and%20Investment%20Fund%20Shares/Investment%20Fund%20Shares.md#value-of-the-interest).

### Mutual Funds

Conventional mutual fund shares are bought or redeemed at the fund's next calculated net asset value per share, rather than at continuously changing trading prices.

This comparison concerns trading and redemption arrangements. It does not determine whether the fund holds stocks, bonds, or other assets.

## Privately Offered Funds

Private funds generally can be bought only by investors who meet eligibility rules, such as minimum wealth or income. When investors can withdraw their money depends on the fund.

The following are common private-fund categories, not a complete classification of private investing.

### Hedge Funds

A hedge fund is a private pooled investment fund, generally available only to eligible investors. It may use strategies such as borrowing or betting on falling prices and may restrict when investors can withdraw money. Despite the name, a hedge fund is not necessarily a low-risk investment.

### Private Equity Funds

A private equity fund pools investors' money to acquire ownership stakes in businesses. It may buy privately held businesses or buy a public company and take it private.

The fund is the investment arrangement; [private equity](../6.%20Financial%20Instruments/1.%20Non-Derivative%20Instruments/5.%20Equity%20and%20Investment%20Fund%20Shares/Stocks.md#public-and-private-equity) is the ownership interest. Private equity can also be held directly, without a fund.
