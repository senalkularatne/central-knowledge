# Bank Regulation

In the United States, bank oversight is divided among federal and state agencies. A bank's charter is its legal authorization to operate. Its charter and membership in the Federal Reserve System help determine which agency supervises it.

Deposit insurance protects qualifying deposits if an insured bank fails, subject to conditions and limits. Deposit insurance, consumer protection, and supervision of a bank's financial condition are related but different responsibilities.

## United States

### Federal Agencies

- **Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC):** Charters and supervises national banks and federal savings associations.
- **Federal Reserve:** Supervises bank holding companies, meaning companies that own banks, and state-chartered banks that are members of the Federal Reserve System, among other responsibilities.
- **Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC):** Insures deposits at insured banks and supervises certain state-chartered banks.
- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB):** Oversees compliance with federal consumer financial-protection laws for covered financial institutions and products.

### State Agencies

- **State banking regulators:** Charter or supervise state-chartered institutions and share responsibilities with federal regulators.

These roles can overlap. A bank may have more than one federal or state regulator, and the exact arrangement depends on the institution and activity.
