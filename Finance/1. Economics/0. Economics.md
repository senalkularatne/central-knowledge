# Economics

An economy is the set of activities and arrangements through which people make, distribute, exchange and use goods and services. Goods are useful items, such as food; services are work done for others, such as teaching.

Economics studies how these activities are organized, how people choose among competing uses of resources such as time, materials and skills, and who benefits or bears the costs.

## The Embedded Economy

Economic activity takes place within society, and society depends on the natural world. The embedded economy model is a simplified picture of these relationships, used in Kate Raworth's Doughnut Economics teaching materials and the Regenerative Economics textbook. "Embedded" means that the economy is part of these larger systems, not separate from them.

The list below describes these layers in order, from the physical world everything depends on to the human arrangements that lead to finance:

1. **The physical world.** About 13.8 billion years ago, the universe was in an early hot, dense state. Its expansion and cooling are described by the Big Bang model. Much later, stars and planets formed. This is the physical setting of economic life.
2. **Life and needs.** People depend on water, food, materials and energy. What nature supplies, what people know how to produce, and who can obtain it all affect how needs are met.
3. **Minds and choices.** People learn, remember, imagine the future and care about different things. Consciousness means having experiences, such as feeling hungry. Its origins remain debated; we do not need to settle that debate to study how people choose.
4. **Cooperation and rules.** Families, communities, businesses and governments organize how resources are used and shared. Customs, ownership rights and laws shape what people can do and expect from one another. Exchange is one way of obtaining things; sharing and unpaid care also provide people with goods and services.
5. **Arrangements across time.** Someone can provide resources now in return for a promise of repayment later. Such an arrangement depends on the promise being kept, requires one side to wait, and may not turn out as expected. Finance deals with these arrangements, and also with making payments and providing money for activities such as starting a business; see [Obligations and Claims](../3.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities/0.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities.md#obligations-and-claims).

This order is a way to introduce economics. It does not claim that every society developed in this sequence, or that the rules of economics can be worked out from physics.
