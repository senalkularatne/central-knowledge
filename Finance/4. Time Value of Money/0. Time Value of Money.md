# Time Value of Money

Many [promises](../3.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities/0.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities.md#obligations-and-claims) involve money paid at one time and repaid at another. To compare such payments, finance uses the time value of money: the idea that an amount of money is worth a different amount depending on when it is paid. OpenStax *Principles of Finance* calls it "often considered to be the cornerstone concept of the study of finance."

## Why Timing Matters

Money held now can be deposited or lent to earn [interest](../2.%20Money%20and%20Banking/0.%20Money%20and%20Banking.md#interest-and-compounding). When interest can be earned, \$1,000 today is worth more than \$1,000 a year from now, because the earlier \$1,000 can grow into more than \$1,000 by then. Waiting gives up that interest, which is an [opportunity cost](../1.%20Economics/1.%20Basic%20Economic%20Concepts/1.%20Scarcity%20and%20Choice.md#trade-offs-and-opportunity-cost).

So two amounts can be compared only after both are moved to the same point in time. Moving an amount forward is called finding its future value. Moving it back to today is called finding its present value.
