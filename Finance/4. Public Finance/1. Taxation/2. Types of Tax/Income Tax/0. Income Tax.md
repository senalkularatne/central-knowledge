# Income Tax

## What is income tax?

Income tax is a tax on money you earn. The federal government and most state governments charge it. This file focuses on **federal income tax** — the biggest tax most people pay. State income tax works similarly but with different rates (covered briefly in [1. What are Taxes.md](../../1.%20What%20are%20Taxes.md)).

## How federal income tax brackets work

- The US uses a **progressive tax system** — you don't pay one flat rate on all your income
- Your income is split into chunks (brackets), and each chunk is taxed at a different rate
- Only the money **within** each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate

## Common misconception

- "If I earn more I'll move into a higher bracket and take home less" — **WRONG**
- Only the dollars above the bracket threshold are taxed higher, not all your income
- Example: if you're in the 22% bracket, only the income within that bracket range is taxed at 22% — everything below is still taxed at the lower rates

## 2025 Federal Income Tax Brackets (Single Filer)

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|----------------|------|
| \$0 – \$11,925 | 10% |
| \$11,926 – \$48,475 | 12% |
| \$48,476 – \$103,350 | 22% |
| \$103,351 – \$197,300 | 24% |
| \$197,301 – \$250,525 | 32% |
| \$250,526 – \$626,350 | 35% |
| \$626,351+ | 37% |

These brackets are adjusted for inflation each year by the IRS. Married filing jointly has wider brackets (roughly double).

## Example — single filer, \$100,000 taxable income

```
$11,925     × 10%  =  $1,192.50
$36,550     × 12%  =  $4,386.00     ($48,475 - $11,925)
$51,525     × 22%  =  $11,335.50    ($100,000 - $48,475)
─────────────────────────────────
Total federal income tax:  $16,914
Effective rate:            16.9%      (total tax ÷ total income)
Marginal rate:             22%        (rate on your last dollar)
```

Notice: your effective rate (16.9%) is much lower than your marginal bracket (22%).

## What is "taxable income"?

Taxable income is NOT your gross pay. It's calculated:

```
Gross income (W-2 Box 1, 1099s, etc.)
− Deductions (standard or itemized — see Tax Strategy folder)
= Taxable income ← brackets apply to THIS number
```

The **standard deduction** for 2025 is \$15,000 (single) / \$30,000 (married filing jointly). Most people take the standard deduction unless their itemized deductions exceed it.

## Key terms

#### Gross income

- Total money earned before any deductions or taxes

#### Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)

- Gross income minus specific adjustments (IRA contributions, student loan interest, etc.)
- Many tax rules and phase-outs are based on AGI

#### Taxable income

- AGI minus deductions
- This is the number brackets apply to

#### Marginal rate

- The rate on your highest dollar of income

#### Effective rate

- Total tax paid ÷ total income
- Always lower than marginal rate

#### Filing status

- Single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household
- Determines which bracket table and standard deduction you use
