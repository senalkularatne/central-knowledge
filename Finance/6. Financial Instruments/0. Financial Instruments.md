# Financial Instruments

A financial instrument establishes financial rights and obligations between parties. For example, a loan gives the lender a right to repayment and the borrower an obligation to repay; a company share gives its holder an ownership interest.

This section includes currency, deposits, loans, shares, and derivative contracts. Currency is itself a financial asset and is used to pay many other financial obligations.

## Transferring Claims

A claim can often be passed to someone else. The creditor transfers the right to be paid, and the debtor then owes the new holder. In law, this transfer is called assignment. Because claims can change hands, they can be bought and sold; see [Financial Markets](../7.%20Financial%20Markets/0.%20Financial%20Markets.md).

## Securities

Security is a legal classification, not simply another word for anything that can be traded. In the United States, the definition in 15 U.S.C. 77b(a)(1) includes stocks, bonds, and specified options, among other instruments. The full legal definition is longer and has exceptions.

A stock and a stock option can both be securities even though one is non-derivative and the other is a derivative. Securities therefore do not form a third, separate branch beside Non-Derivative Instruments and Derivatives.

In the same way, an instrument connected to a currency can be either kind: a euro bank deposit is non-derivative, while a contract to buy euros at a fixed price next month is a derivative.
