# Options

An option gives its buyer a right, but not an obligation, to buy or sell something at an agreed price. The seller must buy or sell if the buyer uses that right.

## Contract Terms

- Strike price: the agreed price at which the option's right can be used.
- Expiration date: when the option expires.
- Exercise: using the option's contractual right. The terms determine when exercise is allowed.
- Premium: the price paid for the option.

## Calls and Puts

For a stock option:

- A call gives the right to buy shares at the strike price.
- A put gives the right to sell shares at the strike price.

Calls and puts are types classified by the right provided. "Stock option" instead identifies the underlying: it can be either a call or a put.

### Contract Size and Share Ownership

As of 2026, a standard U.S. listed equity-option contract represents 100 shares of the underlying equity. Company events such as a stock split or a merger can change what a contract delivers, so the actual contract terms must be checked.

Owning a call option is not already owning those shares. It is owning the right to buy them under the contract. Owning a put is a right to sell, not proof that the holder already owns the shares.

## Risk

An option can expire worthless, so its buyer can lose the entire premium. Selling options creates different obligations and can involve much larger losses. Knowing the direction a stock will move is not enough: timing and the price paid for the option also matter.

## Pricing

A pricing model is a calculation used to estimate an option's value. Inputs can include the underlying price, strike price, time remaining, expected price fluctuations, and interest rates.

### Options Greeks: Introduction Only

Sensitivity means how an estimated result changes when an input changes. The Greeks are model-based estimates of how an option's price or sensitivity may change:

- Delta: sensitivity of the option's price to the underlying price.
- Gamma: how delta changes as the underlying price changes.
- Theta: sensitivity to the passage of time.
- Vega: sensitivity to implied volatility, the amount of future price movement reflected in option prices.
- Rho: sensitivity to interest rates.

They are estimates, not guaranteed outcomes.
