# Derivatives

A derivative is a contract whose value depends on something else, called its underlying. That may be an asset's price, an [interest rate](../../2.%20Money%20and%20Banking/0.%20Money%20and%20Banking.md#interest-and-compounding), or an index measuring a group of prices. Owning the contract is different from owning the underlying asset.

A derivative can be an asset to one party and a liability to another. The examples below introduce options and futures, not every type of derivative.

## Contents

| Read | Basic relationship |
| --- | --- |
| [1. Options](1.%20Options.md) | The buyer receives a right; the seller accepts a corresponding obligation. |
| [2. Futures](2.%20Futures.md) | Both parties take on obligations to buy or sell at an agreed price on a future date, under a contract with standard terms. |

Options come first because the Futures note compares an option buyer's right with the obligations both sides of a futures contract have.

## Other Forms

- Forwards: individually negotiated agreements for a future transaction.
- Swaps: contracts to exchange payments according to agreed rules.

These are further examples, not a complete list of derivatives.
