# Stocks

A stock represents ownership in a company; a share is a unit of that ownership. In this notebook, "company shares" and "stocks" mean the same thing.

Ownership can produce a [return](../../../5.%20Risk%20and%20Return/1.%20Single%20Investments.md#return) through changes in share price and through dividends, which are distributions a company makes to shareholders. Neither a rising price nor a dividend is guaranteed.

## Share Rights

A share class is a group of shares with specified rights. Common and preferred describe different rights, not whether the company is publicly traded.

- Common Stock: often carries voting rights and a claim on what remains after the company's obligations and prior claims are met.
- Preferred Stock: generally has priority over common stock for specified distributions and usually has limited or no voting rights. Payment and voting terms vary; a preferred dividend is not automatically guaranteed.

A company's documents set out the exact rights of each share class.

## Public and Private Equity

Publicly traded shares can be bought and sold in public markets.

Private equity is ownership in companies whose shares are not publicly traded. Investors may own it directly or through a fund. Selling such an investment can be much harder than selling a publicly traded stock.

A share can be both private and preferred, or both public and common. Public or private describes whether the shares trade in public markets; common or preferred describes the rights they carry.

## REITs and ADRs

- A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. This describes an organization, not a share class. Some REITs are publicly traded; others are not.
- An American Depositary Receipt (ADR) is how the stocks of most foreign companies are traded in U.S. markets. A U.S. depositary bank issues it, and each ADR represents one or more shares of a foreign company's stock, or a fraction of a share. An ADR is not a common or preferred share class.
