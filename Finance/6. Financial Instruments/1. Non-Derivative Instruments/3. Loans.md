# Loans

A loan agreement supplies money from a lender to a borrower who agrees to repay it. The lender is the creditor and the borrower is the debtor; see [Obligations and Claims](../../3.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities/0.%20Assets%20and%20Liabilities.md#obligations-and-claims).

William Goetzmann, author of *Money Changes Everything*, calls a loan "the simplest kind of financial technology": "I borrow money today and I pay it back tomorrow." In his view, finance "requires that there be some passage of time between the beginning of a contract or agreement and the fulfillment of the agreement."

This note covers ordinary, non-negotiable loan agreements, meaning agreements not designed to trade as securities between investors. Borrowing through debt securities is a different instrument form in this notebook.

## Loan Terms

- Principal: the original amount lent, or the amount of it still outstanding.
- [Interest](../../2.%20Money%20and%20Banking/0.%20Money%20and%20Banking.md#interest-and-compounding): the charge for using the borrowed money.
- Repayment schedule: when payments are due and what they cover.
- Maturity: when the remaining amount is due under the agreement.

The contract may also require fees. Default means failing to meet a required obligation, such as making a payment when due.

Some loans also require collateral: property a borrower pledges for the loan. If the borrower does not repay, the lender can seize it and sell it to cover the debt. For example, a home is usually the collateral for a loan used to buy it.

## Two Sides of the Agreement

The lender holds a repayment claim, which is an asset. The borrower has a liability. A promise to repay is not a guarantee that repayment will occur.

## Records

A promise to be kept later has to be remembered, so people record who owes what. A clay tablet from Nippur, in Mesopotamia, now in the Penn Museum, records a loan of barley "bearing interest." It names the lender and the borrower and says: "At the delivery of the harvest, the barley and its interest he will measure out." Five people are named as witnesses, the last of them a scribe, and the tablet is dated by a year of King Hammurabi's reign. It belongs to the Old Babylonian period, about 1900 to 1600 BCE.

It is one surviving record of a loan, not the first, and it does not show that finance began with writing. Today the same job is done by signed contracts, bank statements and electronic records.
