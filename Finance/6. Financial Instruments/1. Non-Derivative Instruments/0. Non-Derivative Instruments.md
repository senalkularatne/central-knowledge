# Non-Derivative Instruments

This folder covers ordinary forms of money, repayment claims, and ownership interests. Unlike a derivative, whose value depends on the price of something else, each of these instruments is itself the money, the claim or the ownership.

The notes are sorted by the form the instrument takes. The forms come from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) classification used in official financial statistics, and only the forms a beginner needs are included.

Currency and bank deposits come first because they are the forms money takes. Loans come before debt securities because a debt security is a loan designed to be sold between investors, so loan terms such as principal and interest are explained first. Equity comes last so that owning part of a company can be compared with lending to it.

## Cash Terminology

IAS 7 is the international accounting standard for cash flow statements, which report the cash coming into and going out of a business. Under IAS 7, cash includes physical cash and demand deposits, meaning bank deposits that can be withdrawn at any time. Currency and deposits are still different instrument forms, so they have separate notes here.

Cash equivalents are certain short-term investments that can readily be converted into a known amount of cash with insignificant risk of a change in value. "Cash equivalent" is a label for investments that meet these conditions, not a separate kind of instrument.

Non-derivative does not mean spendable cash: ordinary stocks and bonds also belong in this folder.

## Classification Boundaries

Debt is broader than debt securities. Bank deposits are also debt, because the bank owes the money to the account holder, and loans are debt too. The IMF's broad definition of debt includes currency and deposits, so cash and debt are not separate, non-overlapping categories.

Some instruments combine features. For example, a convertible bond is a bond that the holder can exchange for the company's shares, so it has features of both debt and equity. Its terms, not its name, decide how it is classified.
