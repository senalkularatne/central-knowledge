# Factors affecting SPike

## 1. Sudden Demand of a thing in shortage

1. Crude Oil / Fuel (2026 Strait of Hormuz Closure & 1973 OPEC Embargo)
• The Shock: The March 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz triggered the largest oil supply shock on record, knocking out 10.1 million barrels per day (roughly 8% of global supply). This bypassed the historic 1973 OPEC embargo and the 1979 Iranian Revolution in scale.
• The Demand Surge: Fuel is the ultimate "panic" commodity. A sudden threat to supply triggers immediate, massive hoarding by consumers fearing grid locks, alongside frantic buying from governments trying to secure strategic reserves. The 2026 shock forced nations to release a record 400 million barrels from strategic stockpiles to stabilize markets.


Semiconductors / Computer Chips (2020–2023)
• The Shock: The COVID-19 pandemic forced factory shutdowns, which were later compounded by a major factory fire at Renesas in Japan and severe droughts in Taiwan (as chip manufacturing requires massive amounts of pure water).
• The Demand Surge: Lockdowns forced hundreds of millions of people worldwide to suddenly work and study from home, triggering an overnight, unprecedented spike in demand for laptops, webcams, gaming consoles, and home networking equipment. Combined with automotive tech shifts, lead times for chips stretched past 26 weeks, stalling the manufacturing of over 9.5 million vehicles in 2021 alone and costing the global economy billions.


Medical Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) (2020)
• The Shock: Early 2020 lockdowns halted factory production primarily based in China, while global logistics routes completely collapsed.
• The Demand Surge: Virtually overnight, the global demand for N95 masks, surgical gowns, and gloves shifted from standard hospital procurement to billions of citizens and every medical facility on earth seeking them simultaneously. Demand multiplied by thousands of percentage points within weeks, leading to severe bidding wars, hoarding, and counterfeit markets.


### How to predict this

If you can predict this, then you can decide which company will capitalize on this more, then you invest in that company if their stock is low and likely to surge.
