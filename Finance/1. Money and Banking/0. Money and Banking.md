# Money and Banking

This area covers what money is, how its value changes, and how banks and central banks affect the movement and supply of money.

## Money

Money is something people generally accept as payment. It serves as:

- A medium of exchange: something used to pay for goods and services.
- A unit of account: a common way to express and compare prices.
- A store of value: something that can be held for later spending, although its purchasing power can change.

Money can take the form of physical currency, such as banknotes and coins, or balances held in bank accounts. A bank deposit is money the bank owes the account holder. The detailed differences between these forms are covered later in Financial Instruments.

## Interest and Compounding

Interest is the amount paid for the use of borrowed money. It is a cost to the borrower and income to the lender. A bank may also pay interest on a deposit.

An interest rate expresses that payment relative to the amount on which it is calculated, for a stated period. Compounding means that interest is calculated on accumulated interest as well as on the original amount. Compounding can grow savings, but it can also grow an unpaid debt.

## Inflation

Inflation is a rise in the general level of prices over time. The same amount of money then buys less, so its purchasing power falls. One expensive product does not by itself establish inflation across the economy.

If inflation slows but remains positive, prices are still rising, just more slowly. Falling prices are different from slower price increases.

## Exchange Rates

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. For example, if one euro costs \$1.10, then \$110 buys 100 euros. If a euro later costs \$1.20, those 100 euros are worth \$120.

Inflation changes how much money buys in goods and services. An exchange rate shows how much it buys in another currency.

Exchange rates are one topic of international finance, the study of money moving between countries. People, companies, and governments can all deal across borders, so international finance overlaps all three branches in the [Notebook Map](../0.%20Finance.md#notebook-map) rather than being a fourth.

## Banking

A commercial bank accepts deposits, makes loans, and provides payment services. A loan supplies money to a borrower who agrees to repay it.

A central bank performs different functions, such as influencing interest rates and the supply of money and supporting the banking system. The Federal Reserve is the central bank of the United States. Its monetary policy is the set of actions it takes to influence monetary and financial conditions.
