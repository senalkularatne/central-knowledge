# Finding a Surgeon

## Research Methodology (read first)

The core mistake to avoid: for **every** well-known surgeon there is at least one detailed, angry,
convincing thread claiming they butcher patients - and at least one glowing one. The *existence* of
either is close to zero signal, because the base rate is ~100%. Ranking a surgeon up or down off a
single thread (however detailed) is the internet trap. Sentiment is a source of *questions*, never a
verdict.

**Evidence hierarchy - what actually counts, strongest first:**

1. **Volume of independent, patient-posted 12-month photo journeys.** Judge the *distribution*
   across many cases (especially cases like yours - same Norwood stage, hair type, skin), not one
   hero result. Actively hunt for the surgeon's *worst* results and *revision* cases.
2. **The surgeon's own primary answers** to specific technical questions, in writing, at consult
   (density in FU/cm2, how they set angle and direction, your candidacy, donor plan). Evasion is
   itself data.
3. **Verifiable facts:** board / ABHRS / ISHRS credentials, whether they personally do hairline
   design + extraction + incisions, pricing structure, years and case volume, published technique
   work.
4. **Forum and review sentiment (weakest):** generates leads to investigate, nothing more.

**Rules for handling sentiment (both directions):**

- A concern (or a rave) only graduates from noise to signal when it is **corroborated** - multiple
  *unrelated* patients independently documenting the *same specific* thing - **documented**
  (screenshots, the surgeon's own words, photos), and **consistent over time**.
- Discount by conflict of interest and account quality on *both* sides: paid directories and
  sponsored forums (positive bias); anonymous single-purpose or serial-reposting accounts
  (negative bias).
- Correct for volume and negativity bias: unhappy patients post loudest; silence is not quality; a
  smaller or newer surgeon simply has less signal, not necessarily worse outcomes.
- Never let one source produce a verdict. One source = one data point = a lead.

**Decision rule - only promote a name to [Best Surgeon.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Research/Best%20Surgeon.md) when ALL hold:**

- several independent 12-month journeys (target 5+) including cases similar to yours;
- a direct consult with satisfactory, specific technical answers;
- no *corroborated, documented, repeated* ethical red flag (a single thread does not qualify);
- clear fit to your Norwood stage and donor, scored against
  [Good Hai Transplant.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Good%20Hai%20Transplant.md).

Absence of a bad thread is not a criterion; presence of one is only a prompt to investigate. Track
all candidate names and metadata in
[Surgeon Registry.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Research/Surgeon%20Registry.md); work the case
for/against each in [Surgeons Scratchpad.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Research/Surgeons%20Scratchpad.md).

## Sponsored Reviews

- Be cautious of sponsored reviews and verify the credentials of the surgeon independently.
- Look for reviews from multiple sources, including forums, social media, and third-party review sites.
- Use own judgment and consult multiple sources before making a decision.
- **Assume every curated "best surgeon" list is paid.** The r/HairTransplants moderator states
  that **IAHRS (Spencer Kobren), Hair Restoration Network (Melvin Lopez), Joe Tillman /
  Hair Transplant Mentor, and Spex** all take fees from the surgeons they promote. IAHRS looks
  like a medical board but is a **paid membership** — useful as a starting point, not a verdict.
- **Ignore Google, Yelp, Trustpilot, and RealSelf ratings** — they are trivially astroturfed.
  Clinics have been caught running fake reviews and paying for testimonials.
- **The real signal is independent, documented 12-month patient journeys** on r/HairTransplants,
  HairRestorationNetwork, HairLossExperiences, and YouTube — posted by patients, not the clinic.
- Beware paid influencers and "advisors" who funnel you to a specific clinic for a referral fee.

## Previous Work and Success Rates

- **Demand independent 12-month results**, not the clinic's own before/after gallery (cherry-picked).
  Hair keeps maturing to ~12 months, so anything shorter overstates the outcome.
- **Confirm the surgeon personally performs the key steps** — hairline design, graft extraction,
  and recipient-site incisions. Ask exactly which steps technicians do. Technician-run "mills"
  are where most botches happen.
- **Graft survival should be >90%.** Ask how they protect grafts (out-of-body time, storage) and
  how they manage the **donor area** so the back/sides don't end up thin or patchy (see
  [Good Hai Transplant.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Good%20Hai%20Transplant.md)).
- **Be wary of megasessions (>3,000 grafts).** The donor supply is finite and overharvesting is
  permanent; a natural result usually means planning for 2–3 conservative sessions over time.
- **Consult at least two surgeons.** Match each against your Norwood stage, donor density, and
  realistic long-term plan. If you are not fully comfortable, walk away — no transplant beats a bad one.
- Match technique to your case: FUE tends to suit younger / less-advanced loss; FUT can give
  higher yield for older / more-advanced loss. Decide *with* the surgeon, not from an ad.

## Reputation

- Be wary of controlled reputation https://www.reddit.com/r/HairTransplants/comments/171jcu4/is_konior_worth_35k_an_indepth_analysis_from_a_2x/
- **Watch how a surgeon reacts to criticism** — this is the clearest tell for the
  "gaslights / won't admit mistakes / screws over patients" pattern:
  - Legal threats or cease-and-desist letters to silence negative reviews (a documented 2026
    pattern for at least one US surgeon) are a hard red flag.
  - Refusing to acknowledge a poor result, or blaming the patient, is disqualifying.
  - Aggression toward reviewers or critics (up to reported threats) has surfaced for some names.
- **Cross-reference list membership against the forums.** Some IAHRS-listed surgeons are
  simultaneously flagged or suspended elsewhere (e.g., suspended from HRN, or under an open
  community investigation) — proof that a paid listing is not a safety guarantee.
- Check whether a clinic's positive reviews are concentrated on astroturf-prone sites vs. spread
  across independent forums with full photo timelines and post histories.
