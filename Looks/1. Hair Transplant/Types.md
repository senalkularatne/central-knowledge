# Types

Both are legitimate. The technique matters less than the surgeon doing it well; forums warn against
picking a method (or clinic) off marketing. Decide with the surgeon based on your Norwood stage,
donor density, and how much coverage you need.

## FUE

*Follicular Unit Excision (Extraction).* Individual follicular units are punched directly from the
donor area, one at a time, then placed.

- **Scarring:** Tiny dot scars scattered across the donor; no linear scar. Lets you wear hair very short.
- **Recovery:** Generally faster; no strip suture line.
- **Best for:** Younger or less-advanced loss, smaller/targeted sessions, people who want to buzz short.
- **Watch-outs:** Slower and more expensive per graft; **overharvesting the donor is a real risk** and
  is permanent (see the "safe donor area" note in
  [Good Hai Transplant.md](1.%20Hair%20Transplant/Good%20Hai%20Transplant.md)). Often the format used by
  high-volume technician "mills" - confirm the surgeon does the extractions and incisions.

## FUT

*Follicular Unit Transplantation (strip method).* A strip of scalp is removed from the donor, closed
with sutures, and dissected into grafts under magnification.

- **Scarring:** A single linear scar across the back, hidden by hair of medium length or longer.
- **Yield:** Often the **highest graft yield** and can preserve the best donor hair for large coverage.
- **Best for:** Older or more-advanced loss needing maximum grafts, people who keep their hair longer.
- **Watch-outs:** Linear scar limits how short you can shave the back; slightly longer initial recovery.

> Many top surgeons offer **both** and will recommend based on your case - a surgeon who only pushes
> one method for everyone is a mild flag. Plan realistically for **2-3 sessions** over time rather than
> one megasession, since the donor supply is finite.
