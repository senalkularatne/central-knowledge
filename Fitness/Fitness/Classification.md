# Classification

## By Effect

Physical exercises are generally grouped into three types, depending on the overall effect they have on the human body.

### Aerobic Exercise

- Aerobic exercise is any physical activity that uses large muscle groups and causes the body to use more oxygen than it would while resting.
- The goal of aerobic exercise is to increase cardiovascular endurance.
- Examples of aerobic exercise include running, cycling, swimming, brisk walking, skipping rope, rowing, hiking, dancing, playing tennis, continuous training, and long distance running.

### Anaerobic Exercise
- Anaerobic exercise is any physical activity that involves short bursts of intense effort where the body's demand for oxygen exceeds the oxygen supply available.
- The goal of anaerobic exercise is to increase muscle strength, power, and mass.
- Examples of anaerobic exercise include weight lifting, sprinting, high-intensity interval training (HIIT), and plyometric exercises.

### Flexibility Exercise

- Flexibility exercises stretch and lengthen muscles.
- Activities such as stretching help to improve joint flexibility and keep muscles limber. The goal is to improve the range of motion which can reduce the chance of injury.

## Dynamic and Static Exercise

- Types of exercise can also be classified as dynamic or static. 
- 'Dynamic' exercises such as steady running, tend to produce a lowering of the diastolic blood pressure during exercise, due to the improved blood flow. 
- Conversely, static exercise (such as weight-lifting) can cause the systolic pressure to rise significantly, albeit transiently, during the performance of the exercise.
- In summary, dynamic exercises generally improve cardiovascular function and endurance, while static exercises primarily enhance muscle strength and stability. Both types of exercise have their unique benefits and can be incorporated into a balanced fitness regimen.
- It is important to note that a well-rounded fitness program typically includes both dynamic and static exercises to ensure comprehensive development of cardiovascular health, muscular strength, flexibility, and overall physical performance.
- Examples of dynamic exercises include running, cycling, swimming, and aerobic dance, while examples of static exercises include weight lifting, planking, and yoga poses that require holding a position for a period of time.
- It is also worth mentioning that some exercises can have both dynamic and static components. For example, yoga often involves holding poses (static) while transitioning between them (dynamic), providing a combination of benefits from both types of exercise.
- Understanding the distinction between dynamic and static exercises can help individuals design a balanced workout routine that maximizes the benefits of both types of exercise, contributing to overall fitness and health.
