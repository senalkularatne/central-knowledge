Political scientists describe governments using four different layers at the same time. These layers are:

- **Level of centralization**: The distribution of power between the central government and regional or local authorities, such as federalism or unitary systems.
- **Degree of political freedom**: The extent to which citizens have political rights and civil liberties, ranging from full political freedom to limited or no political freedom.
- **Form of government**: The structure and organization of the government, such as democracy, monarchy, or authoritarianism.
- **Political ideology**: The set of beliefs and values that guide the government's policies and actions, such as conservatism, liberalism, or socialism.


Also, descriptions of any government always depend on the specific time period in history, because a nation's structural system can change over time.

# Degree of Political Freedom
Political scientists look at political freedom in two different ways: by its legal structure (how it is written down) and by its actual result (how it plays out in real life).

## 1. Legal Structure
If we look at the legal structure of how a government handles political freedom, there are two primary options:

- **Constitutional:** The government's power is strictly limited by a supreme set of laws to protect individual rights and political freedoms.
- **Arbitrary / Absolute:** The government has unlimited, unchecked power. There is no supreme law to stop rulers from taking away political freedoms at any moment.

## 2. Actual Results (Real-World Freedom)
If we look at how political freedom actually plays out in real life, countries are generally categorized into three groups:

- **Free (Full Freedom):** Systems with strong constitutional protections where citizens openly voice opinions, vote in competitive elections, form opposition parties, and hold leaders accountable without fear of retaliation (e.g., United States, Germany).
- **Partly Free (Limited Freedom):** Systems that feature basic voting structures but suffer from systemic issues like political corruption, heavy media censorship, institutional weakness, or uneven protections for individual civil rights (e.g., Mexico, Ukraine).
- **Not Free (No Freedom):** Authoritarian structures where fundamental civil rights are absent, political opposition is outlawed or severely suppressed, elections are rigged or non-existent, and the ruling group controls independent speech and organizations (e.g., China, North Korea).
