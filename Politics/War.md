# War

## Common Causes of War

- Territorial disputes
- Resource competition
- Political and ideological conflicts
- Nationalism and ethnic tensions
- Power struggles and regime changes

## Types of War

- Proxy War
- Civil War
- Total War
- Limited War
- Asymmetric War
- World War - A large-scale war involving multiple nations across the globe, typically characterized by widespread destruction and significant geopolitical impact.

## Weapons of War

- Firearms
- Artillery
- Tanks and Armored Vehicles
- Aircraft
- Naval Vessels
- Missiles and Rockets
- Chemical and Biological Weapons
- Nuclear Weapons

## Rules of War

- Distinction between combatants and non-combatants
- Proportionality in the use of force
- Treatment of prisoners of war
- Protection of civilians and civilian infrastructure
- Prohibition of certain weapons and tactics (e.g., chemical weapons, torture)
- Adherence to international humanitarian law and conventions
- Follow international laws when dealing with locals and occupied territories

## Guerilla Warfare

Guerilla warfare is a form of irregular warfare in which small groups of combatants use military tactics, such as ambushes, sabotage, raids, and hit-and-run strategies, to fight a larger and less-mobile traditional military. It is often employed by insurgent forces against established governments or occupying powers.

• Chinese Communist Revolution (1927–1949): Led by Mao Zedong, the People's Liberation Army used rural base areas, peasant mobilization, and protracted mobile guerrilla warfare to defeat the better-equipped Nationalist government (Kuomintang) and establish the People's Republic of China.
• Cuban Revolution (1953–1959): Fidel Castro, Che Guevara, and the 26th of July Movement launched a small-scale guerrilla campaign from the Sierra Maestra mountains, growing from a handful of survivors to collapse the U.S.-backed Batista dictatorship.
• Vietnam War / Second Indochina War (1955–1975): The Viet Cong and North Vietnamese forces combined conventional military strategy with persistent southern guerrilla insurgency to exhaust and defeat U.S. and South Vietnamese military capabilities.
• Yugoslav Partisans (1941–1945): Led by Josip Broz Tito, this World War II resistance movement tied down massive Axis occupation forces and successfully liberated Yugoslavia largely through their own asymmetric mountain warfare efforts.
• Soviet-Afghan War (1979–1989): The Afghan Mujahideen used rugged mountain terrain, ambush tactics, and foreign-supplied anti-aircraft weaponry to inflict unsustainable losses on the invading Soviet military, forcing a total withdrawal.
